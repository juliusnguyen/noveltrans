The plan predicted the hardcoded `376 // count` was under-sampling the cross-check novel.
It was: novel 2302601602 has **1811 chapters**, so the old code sampled only its first
fifth and every request past that 404'd, silently swallowed. That is why 070's second novel
contributed "+1 and +2 new mappings".

### The table

Rebuilt from 60 chapters of 2608569069 plus 15 each of 2302601602 and 2201601601:
**140 mappings, zero conflicts**, and all three novel pairs agree on every shared key

Worth recording for anyone tempted to fix this by sampling alone: the first widening
(28 → 52 chapters) bought only 6 mappings. The tail is long and thin, which is exactly why
the retry is the fix and the table is only its cost lever.

### The repair invalidates translations unconditionally

The plan's central call, and the one that makes this more than a re-download button. The
damage is **not detectable in the translation**: here the model emitted `渺`, a plausible Han
character absent from the source, and it could as easily have emitted a plausible Vietnamese
word no scan could find. So residue in `content` means the translation derived from it is
untrustworthy, full stop.

Audio goes with it, per the plan's correction (D): `pending_audio` re-queues on an empty
path or a voice/source mismatch and never on the translation changing, so audio voiced from
the bad text would otherwise survive the repair and be consumed by the video pipeline.

Clearing happens up front rather than per-chapter — one transaction on the GUI thread rather
than writing while the worker writes. The cost is that a failed re-download leaves bad
content with an empty translation, but that state is loud (nothing downstream consumes an
empty translation, and the detector still flags it) where keeping the old translation would
be silently wrong.
