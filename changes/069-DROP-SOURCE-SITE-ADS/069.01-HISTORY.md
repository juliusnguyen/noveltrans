### The predicate

Conjunctive and domain-anchored: a line is dropped only if it is ≤200 chars, **and** a
domain-like token survives de-obfuscation, **and** one of three signals fires (promo cue,
obfuscation, bare domain). The domain conjunct is the safety property — no domain, no
deletion — which is why `"Truy cập vào hệ thống, hắn thấy một dòng chữ đỏ."` (the strongest
cue in the list) is structurally untouchable.

De-obfuscation **deletes** non-alphanumerics rather than replacing them with spaces, the
exact inverse of `tts/clean.py`'s choice. That is what closes `sto9🍀.com` back into
`sto9.com`, and it is safe only because the folded string is never emitted.

### Two things found while implementing, not in the plan

1. **Full URLs were missed.** `_fold` deleted `:` and `/`, gluing `sto9.com` to its path so
   the domain boundary never matched. Fixed by stripping the scheme before folding, keeping
   `/`, and letting the domain regex consume the path — so a bare URL line reads as one span
   and trips the bare-domain branch. Without the scheme strip, `https` alone is five
   alphanumerics and the line looks like it has words in it.
2. **A test of mine conflated two obfuscation shapes.** `sto9🍀.com` (glyph inserted beside a
   real dot) and `sto9。com` (lookalike standing in for the dot) are different mechanisms;
   both are handled. `sto9。.com` — both at once — is **not**, and deliberately so: catching
   it means collapsing dot runs in the detection view, which would fold
   `"Anh ấy nói...com đã nguội rồi"` to `noi.com` and delete a line of dialogue. The test now
   covers the two real shapes and pins the gap with that reasoning.
