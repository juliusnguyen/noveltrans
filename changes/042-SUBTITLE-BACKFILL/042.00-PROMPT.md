# 042 — Recover subtitle timings from existing audio, and write `.srt` without rendering

## The ask

> So I need to re generate all audio to generate the .srt file?
> [...] do all three

Three things, after 040/041 left the feature unusable on an existing library:

1. A **"Tạo phụ đề"** button that writes `.srt` from existing cues — no video re-render.
2. A **backfill** that recovers cues for audio voiced before 040, so ~270 hours of TTS
   does not have to be re-run.
3. Both, because either alone leaves the user stuck.

## Why a backfill is possible at all

`synthesize_chapter` separates chunks with exactly `gap_seconds` of **true digital
silence** (`np.zeros`). Those boundaries are physically present in every file already on
disk, and the chunk list is re-derivable exactly — `clean_for_tts`, `split_sentences` and
`merge_short_chunks` are pure functions of the text and the settings.

So this is a *recovery*, not an estimate. Measured against synthesis with the cues thrown
away: **0.0 ms error on every boundary**.

The proportional alternative (spread a chapter's duration across chunks by length) was
considered and rejected — speech rate is not uniform, and it drifts tens of seconds
mid-chapter.

## Why the button is separate from the render

The `.srt` needs only the segment list and the cues. Producing one through `render_video`
costs **~26 minutes and ~250 MB per part** for a 40 KB text file.

## Open questions for `/plan`

- What makes a recovered cue set trustworthy enough to write, and what to do when it isn't.
- How `tts_speed` affects gap length in a finished file.
- Where the `.srt` must land so a render and this button produce the same file.
- Whether backfill may overwrite cues captured during real synthesis.

## Out of scope

- Forced alignment / ASR.
- Uploading the `.srt` to YouTube.
