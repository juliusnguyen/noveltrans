# 040 — An `.srt` sidecar per part, timed from the TTS itself

## The ask

> let's do the srt sidecar with timings from tts

Following the analysis in the previous turn: burning subtitles into the video costs almost
nothing here (+1.4 % encode, measured), but a subtitle *track* is the better artefact —
YouTube indexes caption text, a typo costs a few KB instead of a 26-minute re-render and a
319 MB re-upload, and full narration text would fight the player-skin layout.

## Why the timings are free

`synthesize_chapter` already splits the text into chunks and concatenates them with a fixed
gap:

```python
chunks = merge_short_chunks(split_sentences(text, ...), ...)
for chunk in chunks:
    samples = self.synthesize(chunk)
    if pieces and gap.size:
        pieces.append(gap)
    pieces.append(samples)
```

Every chunk's exact start and end is known while that loop runs, and thrown away. Capturing
it gives sentence-level timings with **no speech recognition** — and the text is
authoritative rather than an ASR guess at Vietnamese TTS audio.

## The two things that will silently break sync

1. **`_apply_speed`.** `AudioWorker` post-processes the WAV with `apply_tempo` and returns
   `seconds / speed`. Cues captured during synthesis are in *pre-speed* time; unscaled,
   every subtitle drifts progressively.
2. **Chapter offsets inside a part.** A part is several chapters concatenated by
   `_concat_audio`, which inserts no gaps — so a chapter's offset is the cumulative sum of
   the preceding chapters' `seconds`, the same math `build_ass_subtitles` already uses.

## Open questions for `/plan`

- Where cue data lives, and how `synthesize_chapter` returns it without breaking the
  `float` return three callers rely on.
- What happens for chapters synthesised **before** this feature — there is no cue data for
  them, and re-synthesising 75 parts is not an option.
- Whether a part with only some chapters covered gets a partial `.srt` or none.
- Whether the stored per-chapter `seconds` is accurate enough to accumulate across a part
  without audible drift.

## Out of scope

- Burning the narration into the video (`build_ass_subtitles` keeps its title-only role).
- Uploading the `.srt` to YouTube — a separate Studio surface, and this session's record
  with those says it deserves its own change.
- Re-synthesising existing audio to backfill cues.
